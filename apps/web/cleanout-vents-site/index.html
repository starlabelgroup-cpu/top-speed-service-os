Why vent cleaning matters

A clogged dryer vent is more than an inconvenience — it is one of the most overlooked fire risks in the home, and it quietly costs you time and energy on every load.

Lint is fuel

Lint is highly flammable, and it collects inside the vent line where hot dryer air pushes past it on every cycle. Lint buildup in a clogged vent is a leading cause of dryer fires — regular cleaning removes the fuel.

Faster, cheaper loads

When hot, moist air cannot escape, clothes take two or three cycles to dry. A clear vent lets your dryer breathe, so loads dry in one cycle — saving time, energy and wear on the machine.

Know the warning signs

Clothes still damp after a full cycle, the dryer or laundry room feeling unusually hot, a burning smell while drying, lint collecting around the outside vent hood, or the hood flap not opening — any of these means it is time for a cleaning.

Protect the dryer itself

A dryer straining against a blocked vent runs hotter and longer, wearing out heating elements, motors and sensors sooner. Keeping the vent clear is basic maintenance that helps the appliance last.

How often should a dryer vent be cleaned?

Once a year is the standard guidance for most homes. If you do a lot of laundry, have a long vent run, or notice loads taking longer to dry, more often is better — an inspection will tell you where your vent stands.

What are the signs my vent is clogged?

Clothes still damp after a full cycle, longer drying times than you used to need, the dryer or room feeling hot, a burning smell during a cycle, lint around the outside vent hood, or the outside flap barely opening while the dryer runs.

How much does vent cleaning cost?
