Campaign architecture — 1 to 5

Build Campaign 1 first, with its ad groups, responsive search ads, keywords, negatives and conversion tracking. Then let actual lead and job performance decide when Campaigns 2–5 split out.

  1. Campaign 1 · General + “Call Top Speed”The launch campaign: one general structure with appliance ad groups (Refrigerator, Washer & Dryer, Oven & Range, Dishwasher, General), phrase/exact keywords, and Phone Call Leads as the primary goal. Draft built; publishes paused.
  2. Campaign 2 · Refrigerator RepairThe highest-urgency category — food at risk means the fastest decisions. Split out with its own budget when the data justifies it.
  3. Campaign 3 · Washer & DryerLaundry intent with problem-led copy: not draining, not heating, not spinning.
  4. Campaign 4 · Oven & RangeCooking intent with problem-led copy: not heating, won’t ignite, temperature off.
  5. Campaign 5 · Property ManagersA separate B2B campaign — one dispatch, multiple properties, tenant scheduling and documentation — feeding Appliance Pro Commercial Accounts. Commercial work is quoted per facility.

Measurement — the numbers that run Ads

Clicks are a cost; booked jobs are the product. A call only counts as a conversion at 60 seconds or longer — a five-second wrong number teaches Google nothing. Bidding starts at Maximize Conversions with no Target CPA, and the budget ramp ($30 → $50 → $75 → $100/day) is earned by booked-job evidence, not enthusiasm.
